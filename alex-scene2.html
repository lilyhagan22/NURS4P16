Alex’s story · Scene 2 of 3

Before heading home

Alex has received treatment for her heavy bleeding. Her healthcare team has assessed her and says she can return home with a follow-up plan.

A healthcare professional reviews her instructions:

“Monitor for recurrent symptoms and attend your follow-up appointment.”

Alex understands some of this, but she is unsure what “recurrent symptoms” means or what changes she should watch out for. She is tired, thinking about Wren, and worried she will forget something.

“They’ve already explained it. Should I ask again?”
What could Alex say?

It is okay to ask someone to explain that another way. You deserve information you can understand.

Making the plan work at home

The doctor explains the instructions in everyday language and reviews the warning signs and what to look out for with Alex.

Before arranging follow-up, they ask whether anything might make attending difficult.

Alex thinks about the shared car, the distance from home, and Sam’s work schedule.

What could help Alex make a workable plan?

Optional practice

Help Alex find the words

Use Alex’s fictional situation. Do not enter your own health information. Your response is not graded, saved, or sent anywhere.

One way Alex could say it

“I want to make sure I understand what to watch for and whom to contact. We share one car, and getting to appointments can be difficult. Can we make a follow-up plan that works with my transportation?”

Did your response include any of these?

  • What Alex needs explained.
  • A practical concern or barrier.
  • A question about the next step.

There is no single perfect response. The goal is to help Alex communicate what she needs.

A clear plan is a shared responsibility.

Alex’s healthcare team asks her to explain the plan in her own words so they can check how clearly they explained it. Together, they review anything that remains unclear.

Alex leaves with written instructions, contact information, and an agreed follow-up plan. She also knows she can ask more questions.

Back at home, Alex continues adjusting to recovery and caring for Wren. As the days pass, she notices changes in how she feels emotionally.
