A new baby. A new chapter.

Get to know Jordan

Meet Jordan and baby Maya.

Jordan is 22 and became a first-time mom four days ago. She has recently returned home from the hospital after giving birth to a healthy baby girl. She is adjusting to caring for Maya while recovering herself.

Jordan and her partner, Taylor, recently moved to the community. They live in a two-bedroom apartment downtown. She likes asking questions and figuring things out, but learning about local services while caring for a newborn can feel overwhelming. She wants to find support but does not know how or where to start.

During a previous healthcare visit, Jordan felt that her pain was dismissed before she had finished explaining it. She now worries that her concerns will not be taken seriously when she asks for help.

What matters to Jordan

“I want someone to listen to my concerns and help me understand my options.”

Jordan wants to feel comfortable explaining how she feels without being rushed or judged. She values clear explanations and wants to be included in decisions about her own health and Maya’s care.

Her strengths

Jordan is curious and resourceful. She notices changes in how she feels and writes down questions so she remembers what to ask. She often looks online for information and wants to learn how to tell which advice she can trust.

She and Taylor are learning together and talk about how they can support each other. Jordan is close with her aunt, Susan, who calls regularly to check in and encourages her to share what she needs.

Life at home

Jordan and Taylor are still settling into their new community. Their budget is tight, so Taylor cannot afford to take time off work. However, Taylor works irregular shifts and can sometimes be home to help Jordan with Maya during the day.

They mainly use public transportation and taxis to get around. Travelling with a newborn takes planning, and unexpected costs, such as medication or taxi fares to appointments, can be difficult to manage.

Most of Jordan’s family and close friends live farther away. Her aunt Susan is supportive, but she lives too far away to visit regularly. Jordan has not yet found a family doctor or nurse practitioner, but she has kept the contact information provided by her maternity team.

You’ll help Jordan decide what to do next.

Follow Jordan through three situations that involve complications during recovery, conversations with healthcare professionals, and emotional health and wellness after birth. Consider Jordan’s concerns, but also what might make getting help easier or harder.
