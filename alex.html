A new baby. A new chapter.

Get to know Alex

Meet Alex and baby Wren.

Alex is 28 and became a first-time mom four days ago. She has recently returned home from the hospital after giving birth to a healthy baby boy. She is adjusting to caring for Wren while recovering herself.

Alex likes having a plan. She and her partner, Sam, attended prenatal appointments and prenatal classes before Wren was born. She also read all the discharge instructions from the hospital, but interrupted sleep and new responsibilities have made it harder to remember everything.

What matters to Alex

“I want to understand what is normal during recovery and know when to ask for help.”

Alex values her independence and likes clear explanations. She wants to feel comfortable asking questions about her own health and asking questions about Wren.

Her strengths

Alex is good at noticing changes in how she feels and always keeps important information together. She is willing to ask questions when something is unclear.

She and her partner, Sam, talk about everything together. They are very open about what they need and how they can support each other.

Life at home

Alex and Sam live in a two-bedroom home in a rural community, about 45 minutes from the hospital. They share one car, which Sam usually takes to work. Sam returned to work three days after Wren was born because taking unpaid time off would put pressure on their budget.

Alex and her sister, Sarah, are very close, and Sarah calls often to check in. Alex and Sam are friends with their neighbour, Lisa, and she has offered to help out. She is retired, loves babies, and has a vehicle.

Alex is still figuring out what support she needs and how to ask for it.

You’ll help Alex decide what to do next.

Follow Alex through three situations that involve complications during recovery, conversations with healthcare professionals, and emotional health and wellness after birth. Consider Alex’s concerns, but also what might make getting help easier or harder.
