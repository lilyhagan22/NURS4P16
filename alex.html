A new baby. A new chapter.

Get to know Alex

Meet Alex and baby Wren.

Alex is 28 and became a mother for the first time four days ago. She has recently returned home after giving birth and is adjusting to caring for Wren while recovering herself.

Alex likes having a plan. She attended prenatal classes and kept her discharge instructions, but interrupted sleep and new responsibilities have made it harder to remember everything.

What matters to Alex

“I want to understand what is normal during recovery and know when to ask for help.”

Alex values her independence and likes clear explanations. She wants to feel comfortable asking questions about her own health, alongside questions about Wren.

Her strengths

Alex notices changes in how she feels and keeps important information together. She is willing to ask questions when something is unclear.

She and her partner, Sam, talk openly about how they can support each other.

Life at home

Alex and Sam live in a rural community, about 45 minutes from the hospital. They share one car, which Sam usually takes to work. Taking unpaid time off would put pressure on their budget.

Alex’s sister checks in by phone, and a neighbour has offered to help. Alex is still figuring out what support she needs and how to ask for it.

You’ll help Alex decide what to do next.

Follow her through three situations involving physical recovery, communicating with healthcare professionals, and emotional health after birth. Consider both her concerns and what might make getting help easier or harder.
