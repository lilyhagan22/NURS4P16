Alex’s story · Scene 3 of 3

More than feeling tired

It has been one week since Wren was born. Alex is following her physical recovery plan, but emotionally, things feel difficult.

For several days, she has felt sad and overwhelmed for much of the day. Things she usually enjoys feel uninteresting. Even when Sam cares for Wren, she struggles to relax or sleep.

Alex feels guilty because she expected to feel happier. These feelings are making it difficult to manage everyday tasks.

“Everyone says the first week is emotional. Am I supposed to just push through?”

Baby blues

What might it look like?

Tearfulness, mood changes, irritability, or feeling overwhelmed in the first few days after birth.

What is the usual pattern?

Symptoms are generally mild and improve within about two weeks.

What should happen?

Practical help, reassurance, and opportunities for rest can help. Contact a healthcare professional if symptoms worsen, affect daily life, or continue beyond two weeks. You can ask for help sooner.

Public Health Agency of Canada, 2024;
Centre for Addiction and Mental Health, n.d.

Postpartum depression

17.9%

Gheorghe et al., 2021

Postpartum depression

What might it look like?

Persistent sadness, loss of interest, hopelessness, guilt, or difficulty managing everyday activities.

What is the usual pattern?

Symptoms are more intense or persistent than baby blues. Depression can begin during pregnancy or after birth and is not limited to the first few weeks.

What should happen?

Contact a healthcare professional for assessment. Support may include counselling, practical help, and medication when appropriate. You do not need to wait two weeks if symptoms are distressing or affecting daily life.

Centre for Addiction and Mental Health, n.d.;
Vigod et al., 2025

Postpartum psychosis

0.1%–0.2%

Vigod et al., 2025

Postpartum psychosis

What might it look like?

Hearing or seeing things others do not, unusual beliefs disconnected from reality, severe confusion, or marked changes in behaviour.

What is the usual pattern?

Symptoms can begin suddenly, often early after birth, and worsen quickly.

What should happen?

Seek emergency medical assessment immediately. Do not wait for a routine appointment. A trusted adult should stay with the person while another adult cares for the baby. Call 911 if there is immediate danger or safe transportation cannot be arranged.

Vigod et al., 2025

Whatever the number, your symptoms and how they affect your daily life matter.

Which concern should Alex discuss with a healthcare professional?

Struggling does not make you a bad parent. You deserve care for your emotional health, too.

Taking the next step

Alex considers telling someone, but worries about being judged.

“What if they think I’m not a good mother? Sam is already tired, and getting to another appointment will be difficult.”

Sam notices that she seems withdrawn and asks how she is feeling.

What is the most helpful next step?

Asking for support is part of caring for yourself.

Alex tells Sam how she has been feeling and contacts her healthcare professional. Together, they begin a plan for assessment, treatment if needed, and support at home.

Her feelings do not disappear immediately, but she has taken a step toward getting care.

When help cannot wait

If someone may act on thoughts of harming themselves or their baby, or if anyone is in immediate danger, call 911. Do not leave them alone while arranging emergency help.

Possible postpartum psychosis needs immediate emergency assessment. Do not wait for a routine appointment.

Finding support

Your healthcare team

Contact your family doctor, nurse practitioner, midwife, or maternity team for assessment and help planning care.

Health811 Ontario

Call 811 or visit the website for nursing advice and help finding services. This does not replace emergency care.
